Mission 05 — ATLAS · Secondary

ATLAS

How does a cost shock in one industry spread through a global production network?

A local research terminal that keeps observed history, hypothetical scenarios, and learned predictions apart. It runs on one machine. There is no hosted service, no account, and no external call at startup. Data refreshes only when asked.

Release v0.1.2 Code Validation notes

Data

  • Countries. World Bank records for countries and territories, , plus a 2025 cache of records. Each observation carries its source, retrieval date, and missing-value flag.
  • Production network. Seven OECD input-output tables, aggregated to nodes across areas. The networks reconcile within tolerance, with spectral radii under one.
  • Pipeline. Raw downloads go to an immutable hashed cache, then into Parquet and SQLite, queried with DuckDB and served by FastAPI on the local machine.

What it includes

  • Scenario studio. Combined production-cost shocks on the network, solved in PyTorch, with round-by-round transmission and source attribution checked against finite differences.
  • Graph and macro forecasts. PyTorch checkpoints: graph and no-graph models on the network, and compact GRU models ( parameters) with Gaussian outputs. A release check replays them against held-out data.
  • Rates and portfolios. observed daily Treasury par curves, with hypothetical bond repricing. A portfolio studio for uploaded return series covers volatility, drawdown, CVaR, and walk-forward rebalancing. dated Fed and ECB releases sit beside GDP context.
  • Interface. React and TypeScript, with an offline map and accessible table alternatives, in guided and research modes. One local FastAPI process serves the interface and the API.

Results

Retrospective experiments on held-out years for US growth, and one test year for the graph. Not a tradable backtest. Lower mean absolute error is better.

Release v0.1.2 passed backend tests and browser tests, with all checkpoints replayed. CI passed on Ubuntu, macOS, and Windows. An offline check passed with non-local requests blocked.

Limits

  • The GRU does not consistently beat ridge or the training mean: one seed did, two did not.
  • Ridge was best in the graph experiment. The graph version beat the no-graph version on seeds. With one test year, this is feasibility evidence only.
  • World Bank history is revised data without vintages, so there is no point-in-time backtest. OECD relationships end in 2022.
