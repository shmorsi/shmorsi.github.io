Where the risk comes from

Intended stock bets 47%Style factors 48%Sectors 5%

Naive signal. Buy the top and short the bottom of three textbook signals, with no risk model. Only 17% of the risk came from the stock bets it meant to make; the rest was nobody chose, and at its worst it lost −88%.

Classical optimiser. Same signals, but a convex optimiser keeps the book to sectors, beta, size and value. rose to 61% and the worst loss shrank to −32%. Return stayed near zero: the signals had no edge ( −0.0039).

Deep risk model. A transformer forecasts the covariance instead of Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage. It predicts risk better ( −2.941 vs −2.899) but hedges worse: the intended share fell to 41%.

Deep views. AlphaFormer, a that compares stocks with each other, replaces the classical signals: rank IC 0.0165 instead of −0.0039, and net Sharpe rose to 0.33.

End-to-end, the primary model. The risk model and a correction to the views are on the portfolio's own net return. Best net , 0.58, but the edge over classical has = 1.70, not significant, and its realised was 0.19: part of the gain is market exposure.

Fully deep. Training AlphaFormer end to end as well made it worse: Sharpe 0.15, FF6 alpha t 0.04. Each yearly refit has at most about 250 monthly decisions to learn from, too few for the extra parameters.

Shaded: the crisis years the project tests on their own, the 2008 financial crisis, the 2020 pandemic crash, the 2022 rate shock. 17% → 61%

Share of risk from intended stock bets, naive to optimised

Ex-ante share of portfolio variance from stock-specific bets, independent Barra-style model, out of sample from January 2008. The end-to-end model keeps 47%.

What it provesMaking the optimiser neutral to sectors, beta, size and value raised the share of risk from intended stock bets from 17% to 61%. Training through the optimiser gave the best net Sharpe, 0.58 against −0.03 for the classical optimiser; the gap is not statistically significant, and adding the deep return model on top did not help.

Approach

How I work

The same four rules on every project. They are why a number on this site means something.

  1. 01

    Decide the test first

    Splits, metrics, baselines and the rule for a disappointing answer are tagged in git before the test set is opened.

  2. 02

    No look-ahead

    Models refit on past data only. Tests fail if changing data after a decision date changes the decision.

  3. 03

    Intervals, not points

    Every comparison carries its interval. When the interval includes zero, the page says it is not an improvement.

  4. 04

    Every number traced

    Figures are read from one data file that names each result's source, and a check fails the build if a page drifts.
