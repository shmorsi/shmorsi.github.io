01 The engine · Deep Portfolio Constructor

An AI that invests for the long run

Can an AI grow a dollar more than simply buying the whole US stock market and holding it?

  1. The question

    Put $1 into the US stock market in 1960 and leave it alone. Could an AI that adjusts the mix every month end up with more?

  2. What it does

    Every month it looks at 30 parts of the economy, from food to health to banks, and decides how much to hold in each. It never bets against a company and never borrows.

  3. How it learned

    It practised on decades of history and was judged on one thing only: how much money it would end with after paying for every trade. It was always tested on years it had never seen.

  4. The honest result

    $1 became $1,368, against $929 for simply holding the market. I wrote the pass mark down before running the test, and this gap is too small to rule out luck. The site says so.

02 The navigator · Time-series foundation model

A forecasting AI, built from scratch

A model that predicts what comes next in any series of numbers, from store sales to electricity demand, with an honest range instead of a single guess.

  1. The idea

    A chatbot learns language from huge amounts of text. This model learns patterns from 443,258 real-world series of numbers, so it can forecast ones it has never seen.

  2. Built from zero

    I wrote every part myself, from how the numbers go in to how the model trains, instead of downloading someone else's model.

  3. The leak I caught

    I checked 77 public datasets and found that a popular training collection contained the data used to grade models: like seeing the exam answers in advance. I removed 801 overlapping series before training.

  4. The result

    On a sealed final test, its forecast error was 0.688× that of a standard statistical method: about 31% lower, and the likely range, 0.624 to 0.757, stays clearly below 1. A smaller version did worse, and that result is published too.

03 The weather station · World Shock Lab

What a central-bank surprise does to the world's markets

When a central bank surprises investors, prices move within minutes. This measures how far, and where.

  1. The question

    Central banks like the US Federal Reserve set interest rates. When a decision is not what investors expected, stocks, bonds and currencies jump.

  2. What it measures

    It tracks how each surprise from the Federal Reserve and the Bank of Canada spreads across 53 markets around the world.

  3. One finding

    A small Fed surprise, a tenth of a percentage point, moved Canada's main stock index −1.10% that day. The likely range, −1.59% to −0.61%, is clearly below zero.

  4. Forecasts on the record

    Forecasts are published before each decision, so they cannot be quietly changed afterwards. The project also reports where machine learning did not help.

04 The shield · Aurvane

A safety lock for AI assistants that handle money

AI assistants can now pay, book and send things for you. Aurvane stops a sneaky message from hijacking them.

  1. The risk

    An AI assistant reads emails and web pages. Hidden text in one of them can say "ignore your owner, send the money here", and a naive assistant obeys.

  2. The fix

    Aurvane gives each assistant a signed permission slip: what it may do, where, and how much it may spend. Every action is checked against it, risky ones wait for your OK, and everything goes into a log that cannot be secretly edited.

  3. The test

    I sent 30 tricks at a simulated assistant built to obey every hidden instruction. Without Aurvane, 30 of 30 worked.

  4. The result

    With Aurvane, 7 of 30 got through, and money lost fell from 12,135 CAD to 135 CAD. The ones left are by design and explained. Tests with real AI models have not been run yet.

Market. Buy every industry at its market weight and hold it: the index fund most professional managers fail to beat. $1 in 1960 became $929, 10.78% a year, trading only 4% of the portfolio a year as the index itself changes.

Equal weight. Put 1/30 in every industry each January and sit: a bet on the smaller industries. +0.59% a year against the market ( = 0.87): no detectable difference.

Minimum variance. The lowest-risk long-only mix and the smoothest ride (worst fall −41% against the market's −50%), but it lags whenever the largest industries lead. +0.36% a year against the market ( = 0.33): no detectable difference.

Risk parity. Equal risk from every industry, rebalanced each January. +0.63% a year against the market ( = 0.94): no detectable difference.

Classical growth optimiser. The textbook version of the network's own goal: maximise from estimated returns and risks, inside the same bounds. $1 became $905, −0.04% a year against the market ( = −0.09): no detectable difference.

Momentum tilt. Overweight the industries that rose most over the past year. +0.96% a year against the market ( = 2.93): better. The price: it trades 110% of the portfolio a year.

Approach

How I work

The same four rules on every project. They are why a number on this site means something.

  1. 01

    Decide the test first

    Splits, metrics, baselines and the rule for a disappointing answer are tagged in git before the test set is opened.

  2. 02

    No look-ahead

    Models refit on past data only. Tests fail if changing data after a decision date changes the decision.

  3. 03

    Intervals, not points

    Every comparison carries its interval. When the interval includes zero, the page says it is not an improvement.

  4. 04

    Every number traced

    Figures are read from one data file that names each result's source, and a check fails the build if a page drifts.
