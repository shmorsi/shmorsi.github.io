01Deep learning · Forecasting · Research engineering

Time-series foundation model, from scratch

Can a small transformer, pretrained from scratch on public data checked for overlap with the benchmark, forecast series it has never seen?

A decoder-only transformer, its tokenizers, training loop and sampling written by hand in PyTorch, pretrained on a free Colab GPU and scored zero-shot with the official GIFT-Eval code. An audit of the pretraining corpus found copies of benchmark series inside it; every one was removed before training. Evaluation rules were fixed in git before scoring.

0.605zero-shot CRPS relative to Seasonal Naive on held-out datasets, down from 0.740 for the smallest model
801pretraining series found to overlap the benchmark, removed before training
10.1Mparameters, every layer written by hand in PyTorch
What didn't work

Prediction intervals are too narrow: the 80% interval covers 65% of outcomes, because long forecasts feed back medians. The first GPU run also diverged in half precision until extreme values were clipped.

02Rates and macro research · Causal inference · Live forecasting

World Shock Lab

How do the world's markets, and Canada's in particular, react when the Federal Reserve (Fed) or the Bank of Canada (BoC) surprises them?

An open-source research platform measuring how Fed, BoC and Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) surprises move 53 global assets. Estimators are validated by Monte Carlo simulation and by replicating published papers; a machine learning benchmark was pre-registered in git; a scenario tool and forecasts published before each central-bank decision sit on top.

−1.10%S&P/TSX Composite on the day of a 10 basis-point Fed surprise, vs −0.19% for the BoC
11 of 13published findings replicated (sign and 90% band)
0 of 80pre-registered regime tests survive false-discovery-rate control
What didn't work

Machine learning did not beat a linear model on 168 held-out announcements: error difference −0.003, 90% interval −0.019 to +0.014.

03Economic systems · Machine learning · Full-stack engineering

ATLAS

How does a cost shock in one industry spread through the global economy?

A local-first economic research terminal. It connects 42,900 World Bank observations for 177 countries and territories with seven Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) input-output tables, solves combined production-cost shocks on a 405-node network with PyTorch, and replays 12 trained PyTorch models against held-out data, all from one FastAPI process on your own machine.

405production-network nodes across 81 areas, solved and differentiated with PyTorch
12trained PyTorch checkpoints replayed by the release gate
44 + 27backend and browser tests passed for release v0.1.2
What didn't work

The neural models did not beat their baselines: ridge regression was best in the 2022 graph experiment, and the recurrent model did not consistently beat ridge on 10 held-out years of US growth.

How I work

Evidence first.

The same four habits run through both projects. They are why the numbers above can be trusted, including the disappointing ones.

Time-based holdouts

Nothing from the future leaks in.

Data are split by date, never at random. World Shock Lab trains up to 2014, validates on 2015–2018 and tests on 2019–2026; ATLAS's graph models train on 2018–2020, validate on 2021 and test on 2022.

Baselines

Every model faces a simple rival.

A neural network against a linear projection and a forecast of no move. Recurrent and graph models against ridge regression, the training mean and persistence.

Pre-registration

The test is fixed before the result.

In World Shock Lab the splits, metric and decision rule were committed and tagged in git before any model was fitted, the held-out test set was scored once, and live forecasts are published before each decision and never edited.

Honest reporting

Null results are results.

World Shock Lab's machine learning test found no gain over a linear model, and ATLAS's neural models did not beat ridge regression. Both say so up front, with the numbers.
