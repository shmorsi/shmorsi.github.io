01Macroeconomics · Causal inference · Live forecasting

World Shock Lab

How do the world's markets, and Canada's in particular, react when the Federal Reserve (Fed) or the Bank of Canada (BoC) surprises them?

Measures market responses to identified shocks with event-study local projections, checked by simulation and by replicating published papers, then turns them into a scenario tool with forecasts committed before each central-bank decision.

−1.10%S&P/TSX Composite on the day of a 10 basis-point Fed surprise, vs −0.19% for the BoC
11 of 13published findings replicated (sign and 90% band)
0 of 80pre-registered regime tests survive false-discovery-rate control
What didn't work

Machine learning did not beat a linear model on 168 held-out announcements: error difference −0.003, 90% interval −0.019 to +0.014.

02Natural language processing · Explainability

Earnings Lens

Which sentences in an earnings release move the stock?

Collected 21,101 earnings releases from 499 S&P 500 companies in the Securities and Exchange Commission's (SEC) Electronic Data Gathering, Analysis, and Retrieval (EDGAR) system and compared a fine-tuned FinBERT model with Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) text features and the reported numbers. Integrated gradients highlight the sentences behind each prediction, and a local Large Language Model (LLM) extracts facts with verified citations.

21,101releases, split by date: train 2016–2021, test 2023 to September 2026
0.0182lower log-loss when FinBERT text is added to the reported numbers (95% confidence interval 0.0133 to 0.0232)
97%of the local LLM's citations valid on 200 releases
What didn't work

FinBERT did not beat TF-IDF on the same input. The best trading signal earned +22.3 basis points per position before costs but only +2.3 after 20 basis points of costs.

03Quantitative risk · Deep learning · Optimization

Shock Lab

What does a macro shock do to a portfolio, and which portfolio limits the damage?

A stress-testing engine for 24 assets and five macro factors. A linear factor model and a PyTorch Mixture Density Network (MDN) turn a shock into a 10-day return distribution; a risk engine reports Value at Risk (VaR) and Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR); a CVaR optimizer builds the portfolio. The live demo runs both models and the optimizer in the browser through WebAssembly.

−17.3%maximum drawdown of the CVaR strategy vs −34.8% for equal weight (2018 to October 2026, after costs)
6.5%95% VaR breach rate on 169 test windows (target 5%)
24 + 5assets and macro factors, daily from 2007
What didn't work

The MDN did not beat the linear baseline on likelihood, and the CVaR strategy's Sharpe ratio (0.53) trailed equal weight (0.63) and minimum variance (0.61).

04Economic systems · Full-stack engineering

ATLAS

How does a cost shock in one industry spread through the global economy?

A local-first economic research terminal: 177 countries and territories with dated World Bank observations, seven Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) inter-country input-output tables aggregated into a production network, a cost-propagation engine, and 12 reproducible PyTorch checkpoints, served by one FastAPI process.

405production-network nodes across 81 areas
12PyTorch checkpoints replayed by the release gate
44 + 27backend and browser tests passed for v0.1.2
What didn't work

The exploratory graph model did not beat ridge regression on its single held-out year, and ATLAS says so in the interface.

How I work

Evidence first.

The same four habits run through every project. They are why the numbers above can be trusted, including the disappointing ones.

Time-based holdouts

Nothing from the future leaks in.

Data are split by date, never at random. Earnings Lens trains on 2016–2021 and tests on 2023 to September 2026; Shock Lab fits each model only on windows that ended before the date it is evaluated on.

Baselines

Every model faces a simple rival.

FinBERT against TF-IDF and the reported numbers. A Mixture Density Network against a linear factor model. A graph network against ridge regression. A neural network against a linear projection and a forecast of no move.

Pre-registration

The test is fixed before the result.

In World Shock Lab the splits, metrics and decision rule were committed and tagged in git before any model was fitted, the held-out test set was scored once, and live forecasts are published before each decision and never edited.

Honest reporting

Null results are results.

Each project publishes what did not work next to what did, and documents the bugs it found along the way, such as a filing-time error in Earnings Lens that placed some price reactions on the wrong day.
