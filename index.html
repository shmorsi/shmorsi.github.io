Market. Buy every industry at its market weight and hold it: the index fund most professional managers fail to beat. $1 in 1960 became $929, 10.78% a year, trading only 4% of the portfolio a year as the index itself changes.

Equal weight. Put 1/30 in every industry each January and sit: a bet on the smaller industries. +0.59% a year against the market ( = 0.87): no detectable difference.

Minimum variance. The lowest-risk long-only mix and the smoothest ride (worst fall −41% against the market's −50%), but it lags whenever the largest industries lead. +0.36% a year against the market ( = 0.33): no detectable difference.

Risk parity. Equal risk from every industry, rebalanced each January. +0.63% a year against the market ( = 0.94): no detectable difference.

Classical growth optimiser. The textbook version of the network's own goal: maximise from estimated returns and risks, inside the same bounds. $1 became $905, −0.04% a year against the market ( = −0.09): no detectable difference.

Momentum tilt. Overweight the industries that rose most over the past year. +0.96% a year against the market ( = 2.93): better. The price: it trades 110% of the portfolio a year.

Deep Growth Network. A trained on the growth of wealth itself, through a that charges for every trade. $1 became $1,368: +0.58% a year against the market ( = 1.40), too small to tell from luck by the pre-registered test. It trades 155% of the portfolio a year and moves a median 81% of the way from its holdings to its target each month.

Shaded: months when the market was 20% or more below its previous peak. Long-only, fully invested, every trade costs 10 bps, out of sample from January 1960 to 2026. Approach

How I work

The same four rules on every project. They are why a number on this site means something.

  1. 01

    Decide the test first

    Splits, metrics, baselines and the rule for a disappointing answer are tagged in git before the test set is opened.

  2. 02

    No look-ahead

    Models refit on past data only. Tests fail if changing data after a decision date changes the decision.

  3. 03

    Intervals, not points

    Every comparison carries its interval. When the interval includes zero, the page says it is not an improvement.

  4. 04

    Every number traced

    Figures are read from one data file that names each result's source, and a check fails the build if a page drifts.
